# ReadTree Research Framework

## 1. Purpose

ReadTree organizes academic literature into a cumulative knowledge tree centered on research questions.

Each paper is connected to the theories, methods, evidence, and scholarly debates relevant to its research question.

## 2. Four-Level Structure

**Level 1 — Research Domains**

Broad areas of academic research.

**Level 2 — Research Questions**

Specific questions and debates within each domain.

**Level 3 — Literature**

Foundational theories, identification strategies, empirical evidence, and recent developments.

**Level 4 — Literature Relationships**

Connections among papers, including theoretical foundations, methodological extensions, supporting evidence, conflicting findings, and cross-domain relationships.

## 4. Paper Classification

Each paper should be classified by:

- Primary research domain
- Secondary research domain, if applicable
- Research question
- Contribution type: Theory, Identification, Evidence, or Frontier
- Data and sample
- Identification strategy or model
- Main findings
- Limitations
- Related papers
- Verification status

## 5. Literature Relationships

ReadTree distinguishes six types of relationships:

1. Theoretical foundation
2. Methodological extension
3. Supporting evidence
4. Conflicting findings
5. Cross-domain connection
6. Subsequent development

Relationships must be supported by the content of the papers. Direct citation relationships require verification.

## 6. Deduplication

Papers should be checked using DOI, normalized titles, author information, and publication history.

Working-paper and journal versions of the same research should normally be treated as one intellectual work.

A paper may belong to multiple domains, but it should have only one primary record.

## 7. Reading Status

ReadTree distinguishes between:

- Recommended
- Saved
- Read
- Reviewed
- Understood

A paper recommended in a reading digest must not automatically be marked as read or understood.

## 8. Current Scope

Version 0.1 defines the research framework and proposed workflow.

Automated retrieval, persistent deduplication, citation verification, and interactive visualization remain future development goals.
